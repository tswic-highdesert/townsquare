Seven hundred and forty one real Russellville listings, plus the town's articles and podcasts. This is built for stumbling onto a place you did not know to look for. Search exists, but it is the fallback, not the product. Everything here obeys one honesty rule: we only assert a business is open when we actually parsed its hours.

Looking for something specific?

Everything above the fold answers "show me something". The random draw sits first because it is the only control that requires no vocabulary at all. Then open right now, which is the one thing on the screen with a deadline attached to it and therefore the most likely to turn into a trip. Then the deliberate browse, then the curated collections, and only then a search field, set as a quiet outline button under a heading that names it as the exception. A search box in the header would tell every arriving resident that the correct way to use this is to already know the answer, which is exactly the behaviour the directory is supposed to replace.

The intro is set in the serif and says a true, specific number. It is the only place on the screen where the product speaks in its own voice, and it is doing a job: 741 and you have been to thirty reframes the directory as a place with a lot left in it rather than a lookup table you have already exhausted.

Nothing is being scored and nothing is being saved unless you save it.

A slot machine withholds. It spins, it delays, it implies you might win. This does the opposite: the next card is already there, visible as two edges behind the current one, and the swap is a 160 ms cross of opacity and a 0.6 deg rotation, the physical feeling of a card being turned over on a table. There is no counter climbing, no streak, no "you've seen 12 today". The footer says plainly that nothing is being scored, because the moment this surface starts keeping score it becomes a game about the app rather than a way to find a bike shop.

Two actions, weighted. Show me another is the primary because the honest expectation is that most draws are a miss, and a miss should cost one tap. Seeing the full listing is the quieter button, which is the correct ranking: stumbling is cheap, committing is deliberate.

Something here is wrong Edit history

Three actions, three targets, three destinations. Call opens the dialler, directions hands off to Maps, website leaves for a browser. Rolling them into one tappable card would make "read the menu" and "start driving" the same gesture, which is the exact mistake the shipped directory card already avoids. Each one names where it goes underneath the verb, so nobody taps Website expecting a phone number.

The provenance block is the part that makes this directory trustworthy rather than merely populated. It says the source, the date, and who touched it, and it is honest that we have no photographs instead of quietly drawing a stock image of some other town's bookshop. It also doubles as the contribution invitation: every line that admits a gap ends in a link that closes it.

The matched substring is marked in every row, which is the cheapest possible way to answer why is this here. Without it, "Farmers Cooperative" under the query "coop" looks like a fuzzy-match accident rather than an exact one. Groups are labelled and ordered places, podcasts, articles, collections, because a person typing four letters into a town directory is looking for a place far more often than an episode, and a single mixed ranking would bury that.

Search is deliberately plain. No recent searches, no trending queries, no suggested terms. Every one of those would be a small surveillance surface, and the product does not need them: the answer to "I do not know what to type" is the stumble button, not a list of what other people typed.

Stale listing notice

We have not been able to reach this business's site since March.

The listing is still here because the phone number worked the last time somebody tried it. That is all we know. It may have closed, it may have changed hands, or the website may simply be gone.

Gold, not brick. Brick is the one alarm register in this system and it is reserved for the river coming up, not for a dead domain. The wording is deliberately about our reach rather than the business's status, because "we could not load their website" and "they went under" are wildly different claims and only one of them is ours to make in public about a neighbour's livelihood.
