Foundations and primitives

The atoms every other section is built from: the palette and what each colour is allowed to mean, the three voices of type, the 4px scale, and the controls a resident actually touches. Nothing here is decorative. If a token exists it is because something ships with it.

Surfaces The background is warm cream. White is a card lifting off it, not the page.
--ts-cream The screen. Every page sits on this. #f8f6f1
--ts-surface Cards and sheets. The only pure white in the system. #ffffff
--ts-stone Image placeholder and inert fill. Never text sits on it. #e5e1da
Ink and its alpha ramp One warm near-black at six opacities, stacked over cream so it reads as a ramp and not six greys.
--ts-ink100% Body text, headlines, anything you read.
--ts-ink6060% Secondary text: bylines, taglines, the lede.
--ts-ink4545% Eyebrows and metadata. Large or uppercase only.
--ts-ink2626% Disabled labels, dot separators, placeholder glyphs.
--ts-ink1313% Dividers and rules.
--ts-hairline11% Card borders. One notch quieter than a divider on purpose.
--ts-ink088% Shadow colour. Not a fill, never a border.
Muted text is ink at alpha, never navy at alpha. Navy is a brand colour, so a 60% navy secondary line drifts blue-cool against warm cream and quietly turns every byline into a link that is not one. Ink at 60% is the same hue as the headline above it, just further away, which is what "secondary" is supposed to look like. It also means one ramp survives a town reskin: when --ts-navy moves to green for the Delta theme, no body copy changes temperature. The inverse holds on dark cards, where the ramp becomes cream at alpha for the same reason.

Ratios computed against the composited colour, not the token's own value: an alpha ink over cream, gold-text over 18% gold over cream. The three that fail at body size have rules attached. --ts-ink45 is never allowed on body copy, only on 11px uppercase eyebrows and metadata where it is a label rather than a sentence, and it is never the only signal. --ts-ink60 at 4.41 misses AA by a hair at 17px, so secondary lines that carry real information (a byline, an address) are set at --ts-ink and only truly ancillary ones use it. --ts-terracotta is a fill colour, not a text colour: on a primary button it carries --ts-surface at 3.61, which passes only at large sizes, so the button label is 15px semibold and gets the same treatment large text does. Terracotta as small body text is banned.

The button radius is 10px and not a pill. Chips are pills because a pill reads as removable, filterable, transient: it is a token you can pick up and put down. A button is a commitment, and it should look like it has edges. The practical version of the argument is that Town Square puts buttons next to inputs constantly, and an input cannot be a pill without its text drifting off the optical centre at either end. Matching --ts-radius-button to --ts-radius-input at 10px means a search field and its adjacent Go button share a silhouette instead of arguing. 10px is also the point where a 44px-tall control still reads as a rectangle rather than a lozenge, which keeps the pill meaning available for chips, where we actually need it.
Elevation Five steps and each one names a distance from the page, not a mood. Shadows are ink at 8%, never black.
--ts-shadow-flat

A card resting on the page. Directory rows, article rows, anything in a list.

--ts-shadow-raised

The hover state of a flat card, and the resting state of anything tappable that stands alone.

--ts-shadow-lifted

A card that has been picked up: dragged, expanded, or the lead story at the top of Today.

--ts-shadow-overlay

Sheets, dialogs, menus. Anything with a scrim behind it. The only step with a long, soft second layer.

--ts-shadow-navy

Reserved for the dark card. A neutral shadow under navy reads grey and dirty, so this one tints with the card.

Brick is never a delete button, so destructive is outlined terracotta. --ts-brick is the single alarm register in this system. It appears when the water plant issues a boil order and when the river is forecast over the levee, and its whole value is that a resident has almost never seen it before. Spend it on "remove creation" and you have taught the town that the loudest colour in the app means a form did something, which is exactly the training you do not want at 6am in a flood. So destructive borrows terracotta but changes shape rather than hue: transparent fill, a full-weight terracotta border, terracotta label. It is unmistakably not the primary button standing beside it, it still reads as consequential, and it costs the palette nothing. Confirmation of a genuinely irreversible action is the dialog's job, not the button's colour.

The cream halo does its real work on navy, where it separates the terracotta ring from a dark field it would otherwise sit too close to. On white the halo is nearly invisible, which is fine: the terracotta ring is carrying it there. Focus is only ever drawn on :focus-visible, so a mouse press does not leave a ring behind.
