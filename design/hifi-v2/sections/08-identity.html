Identity, vouching, and moderation

Signing in, being vouched for by a neighbour, and the light-touch committee that keeps the place honest. Town Square never stores an address, and being verified never means being publicly identified. A vouch is a warm gesture between two people who know each other, so it has to look like one and not like a background check.

The whole design problem of this section is that verification is social, and social gates go wrong in one of two directions. Too heavy and it reads as a background check, which loses exactly the privacy-minded residents most likely to build something. Too eager and it becomes a growth loop that pressures people into asking strangers for favours. Everything below is tuned between those: the ladder never shames a resident at zero, the ask surface has no reminders and no counters, and the only place a chain is ever shown is an admin screen.

What a creation is handed

A resident-built creation runs in a sandboxed frame and asks the shell who is signed in. This is the entire answer, for everyone, forever.

{
  "handle": "westside_ruth",
  "verified": true
}

No email. No id it can join on. No method, no date, no vouch count, no anything a creation could use to build a second identity graph on top of ours. If a creation needs more than a name and a yes, the answer is that it does not.

The shell owns this shape. A creation cannot widen it, and the review reads the manifest to be sure it never asked to.

Revoked. Here is what did and did not happen.

Cascading would be one click and would also destroy the legitimate vouches a bad actor happened to give. In a town this size that is a real cost paid by real neighbours, so the blunt version is not on the screen.

What reporting is not
  • It does not say how many others reported the same thing.
  • It never marks the creation publicly while it is open.
  • It does not tell the builder who reported them.
  • It promises no outcome and gives no status thread.

Every one of those exists on the platforms this is explicitly not. A visible count is a pile-on button, a public mark is a scarlet letter, and a status thread turns a neighbour's quiet note into a case they now have to follow.

The one thing a reporter gets is an email when the report is closed, saying what was concluded. Often that is "we read it, nothing needed to happen".

The whole policy
  1. Town Square follows the law. That is the floor and most of the ceiling.
  2. Code that runs in a neighbour's browser is read before it runs. Every version, including updates.
  3. Nothing is deleted. Things get hidden, reverted, or put back.
  4. Every action is logged with who did it and why, and the log is readable by any verified resident.
  5. We do not referee taste, tone, or who is right about the school board.

Five lines, because a policy nobody finishes reading is a policy nobody can hold you to. Last changed 14 June, and the change is in the log.

Account – what we hold
In plain English

What we hold about you

  • Your handle, @westside_ruth, which the town sees.
  • Your email, which nobody sees. It signs you in and lets a moderator reach you.
  • The date you joined.
  • Three facts about verification: that you are verified, that the method was vouch, and that it happened on 14 July.
  • Which handles vouched for you and which you vouched for. Committee only.
  • What you built and what you changed, with the date.
And what we do not
  • Your address. Not hashed, not once, not ever. There is no column.
  • Your real name, unless you typed it into your handle yourself.
  • Your phone number.
  • Where you are, or where you were. No location, no IP log past the day.
  • What you read. Browsing is not tied to your account.
The postcard method, if a town ever turns it on, touches an address once to mail it and never hands it to us. That is the design, not the promise.

Leaving
Delete my account

Straight answers, so nothing here surprises you afterwards.

  • Your email is deleted. Sign-in stops working immediately.
  • Your verification record is deleted.
  • The vouches you gave are withdrawn. Those branches get flagged for a moderator to read, and nobody is un-verified by it.
  • Your handle is retired, not freed. Nobody can take @westside_ruth afterwards.
  • Versions you wrote stay, still attributed to the retired handle. Town history cannot be rewritten by one person leaving, and that is the point of it.
  • Creations you published keep running unless you take them down first.

The destructive button is the plainer of the two, and the softer path is listed first. Neither is red, because red here would be theatre and the alarm colour belongs to the river.

What this section refuses to build, on purpose. A public vouch count on a profile, which would make verification a status. A "verified since" badge on every post, which turns pseudonymity into a two-tier town. A report count, which is a pile-on button. Auto-cascade on a revoked vouch, which is one click of admin convenience paid for by ten innocent neighbours. And a growth loop on the ask surface, which is the exact move that turns a community platform into the thing everyone left. Every one of those was cheap to add and each is a step toward the app this is explicitly not.
