States, feedback, and motion

Town Square is assembled from a dozen scraped sources that break on their own schedule, read on one bar of signal out past Lake Dardanelle, by residents who did not sign up for anything. Every state below is a real Tuesday, not a footnote: the empties, the ladder of errors, the offline path, the stale timestamp that is the only honest signal we have, and the motion spec that ties them together.

The colour rule for this whole section. Every recoverable problem is terracotta: a field that did not validate, a save that failed, a source that stopped answering. Brick appears nowhere in this section. It is the one alarm register, and if a resident learns it means "your handle is taken" then it means nothing at 6am when the Illinois Bayou is over Weir Road. Sage is reserved for confirmed and safe, gold for civic notice, and the empties get no colour at all except one small accent mark.

Marks for empties CSS only, ink plus one accent, drawn with a pen not a mascot

Rules that keep these from becoming an illustration library. Three shapes maximum per mark, since each is the element and its two pseudo elements. Strokes are 1.5px, the same weight as an icon, so a mark next to a chip does not look like a different product. Ink at 30 percent carries the form, one terracotta stroke carries the meaning, and nothing is filled except a 10 percent leaf. Each sits at a small off-axis rotation, between 2 and 5 degrees, which is the whole difference between "drawn" and "an image that failed to load".

Inline field error message under the field, icon plus text, never colour alone and never a tooltip

Validate on blur, clear on input, never validate while a finger is still typing. The error border is 1.5px terracotta so it is visible against the 1px hairline of a resting field without a colour comparison, and the message is always a sentence that names the actual cause. "Invalid handle" tells a resident nothing. "@kmcbride signed up in March" tells them to pick something else and why. Errors are wired with aria-invalid plus aria-describedby, so a screen reader hears the cause when focus lands, not a beep.

Reconnecting is a toast, going offline is a banner. Losing signal changes what the whole screen means and has to stay visible; getting it back is one moment and then it is over.

What survives offline is a design decision, not a caching accident. The threshold is 18 hours and it is not arbitrary. The Courier posts in the morning; the city posts agendas in the evening. Anything under 18 hours means at most one publishing cycle was missed, which happens on a normal Sunday and is not worth a banner. Past 18 hours a resident is looking at a screen that could be wrong about a road closure, and the product has to say so before they act on it. The banner appears once, at the top of the affected section only, and it is scoped: it never claims the whole app is stale when only one feed is.

The timestamp is never hidden and never abbreviated away. This is the single line standing between "the app is quietly serving you Tuesday" and a resident driving to a meeting that was cancelled. Removing it to clean up a layout is the one change in this system that would be a genuine failure.

Toast behaviour, live stacking, timing, and undo, because a toast you cannot catch is worse than no toast

Toasts land here, bottom left, above the tab bar.

Timing
ToastDismissWhy
info, no action4sConfirming a tap the finger already knows about.

Stacking
  • Three at once, maximum. The fourth pushes the oldest out early.
  • Newest enters at the bottom, older ones rise. The eye is already at the bottom of a phone.
  • Identical toasts collapse and count up rather than repeat: "3 stories saved."
  • Hovering or focusing anything in the stack pauses every timer in it.
  • A problem toast never auto-dismisses, and never gets pushed out by a newer one.

When a banner is right and a toast is wrong. A toast is an event: it happened, it is over, it will leave. A banner is a condition: it is true right now and it stays true whether or not you look at it. Offline is a condition. Stale data is a condition. A feed being down is a condition. All three are banners. Saving a bookmark, publishing a creation, and a failed photo upload are events, so they are toasts. The test is simple: if the statement is still true in thirty seconds, a toast is the wrong shape, because in thirty seconds the toast is gone and the truth is not.

The destructive button is a terracotta outline, not a brick fill. Two reasons, and both matter. First, brick is the flood register and spending it on a delete button is how it stops meaning anything. Second, a filled button is the easy target, and the easy target on this dialog should be "Keep it". The outline makes the destructive choice deliberate to hit without hiding it, and the sentence above it does the actual work: it names what is lost, counts it, and says what recovery would cost. A dialog that only says "Are you sure?" has asked a resident to guess.

Informational sheet, live

What verified means here

Ten neighbours have said they know you're a real person in Russellville. That's the whole mechanism.

Town Square never stores your address, and verified is not the same as identified. A creation you open can see two things about you: your handle, and whether you're verified. Not your name, not your email, not where you are.

  • Vouches are private. You can see your count, not who vouched.
  • You can withdraw a vouch you gave, any time.
  • Unverified residents can read everything. Verified adds posting and building.
Focus and dismissal rules
  • On open, focus moves to the dialog's heading, not to a button. Landing on "Remove listing" is how an accident happens.
  • Tab is trapped between the first and last focusable element inside. Shift+Tab from the first wraps to the last.
  • Escape closes a confirm, an info sheet, and any non-destructive dialog.
  • Escape also closes a destructive confirm. It is the cancel path, and cancel is always safe.
  • Tapping the scrim closes an info sheet. It does not close either confirm, because a stray thumb on a phone should not answer a question for you.
  • On close, focus returns to the control that opened it, every time.
  • The page behind gets inert and stops scrolling. Background scroll under a sheet is the most common way this pattern breaks.
  • Sheets rise from the bottom on a phone and become a centred dialog at 640px and up. Same markup, one media query.
  • One layer at a time. A dialog never opens another dialog; it replaces itself.

Auto-load stops after five screens

Why they differ. In the directory a resident is hunting: they need to know how many are left, they need the footer, and they need to be able to leave and come back to the same place. Infinite scroll destroys all three. In the feed they are browsing, and a button between them and the next story is friction with nothing on the other side of it. Infinite scroll gets a floor anyway: after five screens it stops and asks, because a list with no bottom on a phone means the tab bar and the footer are unreachable, and because five screens is well past the point where somebody is still reading. Both endings are written, never a spinner that quietly stops spinning.

The rule that governs the whole table: motion is never the only carrier of a state change. Every row above has a non-motion partner. The pending pulse is paired with the word "sending". The stale banner is paired with a timestamp that changed colour and wording. The skeleton shimmer is paired with a fill that is visible when it is stopped. Turn every animation in this product off and nothing becomes unknowable, which is the only test that matters, because for some residents it is off already.

Motion, live the same six motions with reduced motion on and off, side by side

Reduced motion is not "everything off". Opacity and colour changes stay, because they are not vestibular and turning them off removes information. What goes is travel, scale, and anything looping: the sheet appears rather than rises, the stagger becomes simultaneous, the shimmer and the pending pulse stop. The press response survives as an opacity change, because losing the feedback that a tap landed is a worse outcome than the motion was.

Focus on every primitive one ring, two rings of offset, legible on cream, on white, and on navy
Quorum court sends the culvert back The Courier · 6:41 a.m.
Tonight at the Center for the Arts 7:30 p.m.

The ring's inner band is cream on light surfaces and navy-dark on the dark card, so there is always a gap between the control and the terracotta. Without it the ring blends into whatever it is sitting on.

Focus is visible for everyone, not only for keyboard users. These specimens render the ring permanently so it can be reviewed; in the product it is :focus-visible, so a mouse click does not leave a ring behind but a Tab key always does. The ring is 2px terracotta with a 2px offset band, drawn as a box-shadow so it never changes layout, and it is never removed, never replaced with a colour change, and never animated. Hit targets are 44px minimum even where the visible control is smaller: the toast dismiss is a 16px glyph in a 44px button.
