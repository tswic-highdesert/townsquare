# ATU Capstone Proposals

Six drafted proposals for the ATU Computing Sciences capstone program. Tal picks
which ones get submitted. Each file contains form-ready answers for the
**Project Idea Form**: <https://forms.cloud.microsoft/r/WcXzRTeueQ>

**Deadline: teams are formed and projects selected late August to early
September 2026.** Selected partners are contacted by mid-September.

Debra Fithen explicitly invited multiple submissions, and selection is
competitive, so submitting three or four independent proposals is the correct
play. Landing one is a win. Landing three is a program.

The "documented need" column maps to the AR-NETWORK Community Health
Needs-Finding Workshop findings that ATU itself summarized: improved access to
information, stronger communication about available services, transportation
coordination, resource mapping, and data collection. Quoting ATU's own needs
list back to ATU is the cheapest credibility available.

## Recommended slate

**Submit 1, 2, and 3.** They cover three different documented needs, span three
different technical profiles so they suit different team strengths, and none of
them carries political or schedule risk.

**Hold 4** unless Donna Smith at Russellville Housing Authority is willing to
co-sign as a second partner, which would make it the strongest proposal on the
list. Transportation is on ATU's own needs list and nobody else in the room is
positioned to build it.

**5 is Tal's call alone.** Strong civic project, real press potential, and it
attaches a surveillance-politics story to Town Square's launch.

**6 only if a fourth submission seems welcome.** It is the least community-facing
and therefore the weakest fit for a program framed around community needs.

## What every proposal says

Two things make these unusually strong against ATU's stated selection criteria
(educational value, technical rigor, feasibility, resource availability, and
alignment with learning outcomes):

1. **Students build on a running platform, not from zero.** Town Square supplies
   the SDK, hosting, authentication, review pipeline, design system, and real
   data on day one. Teams spend two semesters on their actual problem instead of
   spending the fall on scaffolding.
2. **Nothing becomes an orphan.** The partner guide is explicit that students and
   faculty own nothing after the course ends. Every project here is a sandboxed
   creation on infrastructure that already exists and is already maintained, so
   there is no abandoned system at the end. That directly answers the
   "availability of needed resources" criterion.
