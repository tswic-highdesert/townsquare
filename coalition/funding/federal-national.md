# Federal and National Funding for Town Square

Scope: federal agencies and national foundations only. Arkansas state programs,
AR-NETWORK, and ATU internal funds are covered elsewhere.

Everything below was checked on 2026-08-07. Where a date or dollar figure could
not be confirmed on the funder's own site, it is marked unverified and the gap
is named. Nothing here is invented.

## The honest headline

Town Square is a one-person project with a university partner, no confirmed
501(c)(3), and no SAM.gov registration. That combination rules out most of the
federal menu outright, because federal applicants must be a registered entity
with a UEI and most competitive programs assume an organization with a finance
office behind it. The realistic federal path is **ATU as lead applicant with
Town Square as the named community partner**, or **a coalition nonprofit as
lead**, not Town Square applying on its own.

The one exception is European and open-source philanthropy, which funds
individuals directly and in amounts that actually match this stage of the work.

Two structural facts worth internalizing before reading the table:

- **The 2026 federal calendar is mostly spent.** Nearly every relevant program
  closed between March and June 2026. The next real windows are November 2026
  through spring 2027.
- **The local news funders are between open calls.** Press Forward, Knight,
  Lenfest, Democracy Fund and AJP are all closed, invitation-only, or
  geographically ineligible right now. Framing Town Square as local information
  infrastructure is correct, but there is nothing to submit it to this month.

## The five worth actually pursuing

### 1. NSF PESOSE Track 1, with ATU as lead applicant. Target 2027-03-02.

This is the single best substantive fit in the entire federal catalog. NSF 26-506
is the live successor to POSE, and Track 1 exists specifically to fund a team to
**scope and plan the establishment of an open-source ecosystem**, meaning the
governance body, the contributor pipeline, and the sustainability model, not the
software itself. That is exactly the unsolved part of Town Square: a local
community committee governing a codebase meant to be adopted by other towns.
Track 1 caps at $300,000 and NSF expects roughly 30 of them, which is a wide door
by NSF standards. There are no limits on proposals per organization or per PI.

Why it is realistic now. NSF explicitly states the program is not for
well-resourced open-source communities, which is an argument in Town Square's
favor rather than against it. Arkansas is an NSF EPSCoR jurisdiction, locked in
through FY2027 by the CHIPS and Science Act, and EPSCoR co-funds proposals
submitted to regular NSF programs, which meaningfully lifts success rates for
Arkansas institutions. ATU has the registrations, the finance office, and the
tenure-track faculty the PI rule requires. Becky Cunningham or Dr. Robin Ghosh
carries the computing side, Dr. Mikles-Schluterman carries the socio-technical
side, and the socio-technical framing is not decoration in this program, it is
what Track 1 is about.

Why March 2027 and not September 2026. The September 1 deadline is 25 days out.
An NSF proposal needs a PI who has agreed, a department chair who has agreed, an
internal ATU sponsored-programs deadline that has already passed in practice, and
a budget. Rushing it burns the relationship with the exact faculty whose capstone
selection decision lands in the same window. March 2, 2027 gives six months, and
the deadline recurs the first Tuesday of March annually, so nothing is lost.

Confidence: high. Deadlines, tracks, dollar caps and eligibility all came off the
NSF 26-506 solicitation page.

### 2. NLnet, next call opens 2026-09-03, deadline 2026-11-03 at 12:00 CEST.

Grants of €5,000 to €50,000, and eligibility is open to **individuals and
organizations of any type**. That is the rarest property on this whole list: Tal
can be the applicant without a 501(c)(3), without SAM.gov, and without a
university intermediary. Releasing under libre and open licenses is a hard
requirement across all NLnet funds, which Town Square already satisfies.
Grantees also get support services such as security and accessibility audits,
which a one-person project cannot otherwise buy.

Why it is realistic now. The application is short, the money is small enough to
be decidable, and the timing is clean: calls open September 3, which is the same
week the capstone proposals are due, and close November 3. Two months of runway
on a proposal that does not need an institution behind it.

The real caveat. NLnet is paused right now while it transitions from NGI Zero to
the Open Internet Stack programme, and the new fund names (Restack, CodeSupply,
ELFA) have no published scopes yet. Proposals must have research and development
as their primary objective, and EU and associated-country applicants are
prioritised when proposals are otherwise equal. A US applicant is not excluded
but is not favored. Read the new call text on September 3 before committing time.

Confidence: high on dates and grant range, low on whether the new funds' themes
will fit a community information platform.

### 3. AmeriCorps VISTA, sponsored by a coalition nonprofit. FY2027 cycle.

This is the labor play, and it is the one that most directly solves Town Square's
actual bottleneck, which is one person. VISTA places members with a sponsor
organization for capacity-building work under an anti-poverty mandate. Russellville
Housing Authority is the obvious sponsor: it is a public body serving low-income
residents, and "residents cannot find out what services exist" is a textbook VISTA
Economic Opportunity project. Main Street Russellville is the alternative.

Why it is realistic. The sponsor carries the eligibility, not Town Square. VISTA
concept papers are a screening document, not a full proposal, and AmeriCorps
explicitly encourages working with the local Region Office to develop one. Projects
run three to five years, which matches the horizon of a platform that needs
sustained community organizing more than it needs code.

The caveats. VISTA members cannot do direct service, only capacity building, so
they build the coalition and the content pipeline, they do not answer support
tickets. Sponsors pay a cost share and handle monthly member payment reporting,
which is real administrative weight on a small nonprofit. The FY26 concept paper
deadline was March 26, 2026 and has passed. VISTA has historically run multiple
fixed cycles per fiscal year rather than rolling submission, so the FY27 Annual
Program Guidance, typically issued in January, is what to watch.

Confidence: high on the mechanism and the FY26 dates, unverified on the FY27
cycle calendar, which has not been published.

### 4. IMLS National Leadership Grants for Libraries, with Pope County Library as
applicant. Tentative deadline 2026-11-13.

The FY26 program ran $25,000 to $1,000,000 across five grant types, including
Planning Grants and Community-Centered Implementation Grants, both of which run
one to two years. Requests at or under $299,999 do not trigger the 1:1 non-federal
cost share on the National Implementation track. A planning grant to design a
community information platform governed locally, with a rural public library as
the institutional home and ATU sociology doing the needs research, is a coherent
IMLS project and reads naturally in their language of community memory and
digital inclusion.

Why it is realistic. It is the only federal program on this list where the natural
applicant is a small-town institution rather than a research university, and where
the deliverable can be a plan rather than a product. IMLS also has form here: the
retired Accelerating Promising Practices initiative was explicitly aimed at small
and rural libraries around community memory and digital inclusion.

Two caveats, both real. First, **there is no library in the coalition yet**. Pope
County Library System is not on the workshop distribution list, so this requires
recruiting a new partner before it is a live option. Second, IMLS survived a
dismantling attempt and a lawsuit, with a settlement reached in April 2026 and
FY26 LSTA funding at $212.5 million, but the White House FY27 request would cut
the agency from roughly $290 million to $6 million. The House subcommittee marked
level funding at $291.8 million on June 4, and the Senate had not marked up. The
November 13 date is IMLS's own listing but is explicitly tentative, and the FY27
NOFO is not published.

Confidence: medium. Dollar figures and FY26 structure are solid, the FY27 deadline
and the agency's FY27 budget are both genuinely uncertain.

### 5. RWJF Local Data for Equitable Communities, expected January 2027.

This is the funder for the open research question. RWJF's call invites nonprofits
to "collect, analyze, and use data to address inequities in the physical, economic,
and social conditions of a place," and lets applicants define what data is needed
and how it gets collected and put to use by community members. "Quantify what the
loss of a shared local information channel has cost this community" is that call
almost verbatim, and Dr. Mikles-Schluterman is the research partner it assumes.

Why it is realistic. It funds the question the coalition already wants answered,
and the answer becomes the evidence base for everything else on this list. It is
also the rare foundation call where a small local nonprofit is the intended
applicant rather than a disadvantage.

The hard constraint. Applicants must be a 501(c)(3) or 501(c)(4), and
**universities are explicitly not eligible**. That inverts the ATU-as-lead pattern
that works everywhere else: here ATU must be the subcontracted research partner
and a coalition nonprofit must hold the grant. Russellville Housing Authority is a
public body, not a 501(c)(3), so verify its status or use Main Street Russellville
or Co-Create.

Timing. The FY26 CFP launched January 27, 2026 and closed March 3, 2026, with
grants starting July 1, 2026. If the cycle repeats, the window opens late January
2027 and closes in early March. The award amount for this specific call is
unverified.

Confidence: medium. Eligibility language and the FY26 calendar are confirmed on
RWJF's site. The 2027 repeat is an inference from the 2026 pattern, not an
announcement.

## Cheap shots worth taking anyway

These cost hours, not weeks, and can run in parallel with the five above.

- **Sloan Foundation, Open Source in Science.** A two-page letter of inquiry to
  technology@sloan.org, rolling deadline, most projects $50,000 to $600,000. The
  honest read is that Sloan funds tooling, institutions, economic models and
  incentives around **research** software, and Town Square is not research
  software, so the fit is a stretch. But the cost of finding out is one page and
  a stamp, and the "Institutional Support for Open Source" focus area is the
  closest hook.
- **Press Forward.** Get on the email list. The national Pooled Fund is closed
  with no announced date, and only the Colorado and California local chapters are
  currently accepting applications. When the next infrastructure-style call opens,
  the "local information infrastructure, not a newsroom" framing is the right one
  and the 741 loaded Russellville listings are the proof.
- **Mozilla Democracy x AI.** The 2026 cohort closed March 16. Its category one,
  "better information systems including verification tools, diverse information
  sources, and algorithmic transparency," describes Town Square well. Mozilla
  requires functional, user-tested technology rather than wireframes, which Town
  Square has. Watch for the 2027 call. Note the bar that AI must be central to the
  solution, which is currently not true of Town Square.
- **NTIA Digital Equity Competitive Grant Program.** Terminated in May 2025,
  litigated, and in an August 3, 2026 court filing Commerce said it "presently
  targets a December 2026 opening date." Worth a calendar reminder and nothing
  more until it actually opens.

## Too big or too slow for now

On the record so nobody spends time re-discovering them.

- **NSF PESOSE Tracks 2 and 3, up to $1.5 million.** Track 2 requires an existing
  robust product with a real user community and a plan to stand up a managing
  organization. Town Square has one town and one developer. Track 2 becomes the
  right ask two or three years after a Track 1 award, not instead of it.
- **EDA Build to Scale, $500,000 to $5,000,000.** Regional commercialization and
  capital formation. Wrong theory of change entirely, and no EDA-published FY26
  NOFO could be confirmed. The October 25, 2026 date circulating on aggregator
  sites is unverified.
- **Google.org Impact Challenges, $1 million to $3 million.** Both 2026 cycles are
  closed, and the AI for Government Innovation track requires an actual government
  partnership using generative or agentic AI. Russellville city government is not
  in the coalition and AI is not central to Town Square.
- **USDA Distance Learning and Telemedicine.** $50,000 to $1,000,000 with a 15%
  non-federal match, but it funds audio/video equipment, broadband facilities, and
  network hardware for education and telehealth end-user sites. It buys equipment,
  not software or community organizing.
- **AmeriCorps State and National.** Closed March 31, 2026, and AmeriCorps warned
  it may prioritize existing grantees over new awards. VISTA is the right door.
- **NEH Digital Humanities Advancement Grants.** $75,000 to $350,000 by level, but
  the program shows no active deadline and at least one tracker lists it as
  cancelled for FY2026. The community-archives framing is genuinely good. Revisit
  only if NEH posts updated guidelines.

## Dead ends

Stop considering these.

- **Knight Foundation place-based funding.** Knight funds only the 26 communities
  where the Knight brothers owned newspapers. Arkansas has none. The Knight Cities
  Challenge relaunched in April 2026 at $10 million with grants up to $200,000, and
  Russellville cannot apply.
- **NSF Civic Innovation Challenge.** NSF 24-534 is archived with no successor
  posted. This was the best-fitting NSF program by description and it is not
  available. PESOSE replaces it in practice.
- **NSF Smart and Connected Communities.** Every S&CC solicitation back to NSF
  18-520 is archived, and the chain points to NSF 25-543, which resolves to CISE
  Future CoRe rather than a standalone S&CC program. There is no S&CC to apply to.
- **Ford Foundation digital infrastructure.** Ford's own page says it is not
  soliciting proposals. Its Digital Infrastructure Insights Fund core support was a
  tie-off grant ending January 2026.
- **Open Technology Fund.** Rolling and generously funded at $10,000 to $900,000,
  but its mandate is internet freedom under censorship and surveillance regimes.
  A rural Arkansas community platform is outside the mission. Applying would waste
  their reviewers' time and yours.
- **American Journalism Project.** Sources proactively, funds nonprofit newsrooms
  on business and operational capacity. Town Square is not a newsroom, which is
  the correct framing but disqualifying here.
- **PIT-UN Network Challenge.** Members only, and no Arkansas institution appears
  on the member roster. Worth a single email to confirm ATU is not a member, then
  drop it.
- **USDA Community Connect.** Broadband deployment where 10/1 Mbps is unavailable.
  Wrong problem.
- **Aspen Tech Policy Hub fellowship.** $7,500 plus $5,000, requires living in the
  Bay Area full-time for 3.5 weeks. The stipend does not fund the project and the
  residency is incompatible with running the platform.

## Open questions for Tal

1. **Is there a 501(c)(3), and if not, who is the fiscal sponsor?** This is the
   single highest-leverage unknown on the page. It gates RWJF, VISTA, USDA RBDG,
   and most of the foundation world. If the answer is no, the next question is
   whether Main Street Russellville or Co-Create will act as fiscal sponsor, which
   is a conversation with people already in the room.
2. **Will ATU be lead applicant on a federal grant, or only a capstone partner?**
   These are very different asks. Capstone selection is a faculty decision inside
   an existing course. An NSF PI commitment is a multi-year obligation that
   involves the department chair and sponsored programs. Do not conflate them in
   the same conversation, and do not raise NSF before the capstone proposals are
   in.
3. **Which faculty member would actually be PI?** PESOSE requires tenured or
   tenure-track status. Confirm that Cunningham, Ghosh, or Mikles-Schluterman meets
   that bar before building anything around it.
4. **Should Pope County Library be recruited into the coalition?** It unlocks IMLS,
   it is a natural governance home for a community information commons, and it
   costs one meeting to find out.
5. **Is the "what did losing the local information channel cost us" study going to
   happen regardless?** If Mikles-Schluterman will run it as normal scholarly work,
   it becomes the evidence base for every application here at no cost. If it needs
   funding, RWJF in January 2027 is the target and that changes the sequencing.
6. **Is Town Square willing to be renamed inside grant applications?** Several of
   these fund the coalition or the library, not the platform. The grant may be
   titled something else entirely with Town Square as the technology. That is
   normal and usually improves odds.
7. **SAM.gov and UEI registration.** If any federal application will ever name a
   Town Square entity as applicant or subrecipient, start registration now. NSF
   advises new lead organizations to begin at least two months before a deadline,
   and it routinely takes longer.
