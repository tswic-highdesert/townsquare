# ATU Internal Funding Paths for Town Square

Money that flows through or alongside Arkansas Tech University. Scope is
deliberately narrow: ATU-administered programs, ATU-adjacent grants that need a
faculty PI, and federal or state money where ATU is the required university
anchor. External foundations, city money, and county money are out of scope.

Research date 2026-08-07. Every claim below is either quoted from an ATU page or
a named news source, or explicitly marked unverified. Nothing here is invented.

## The finding that reframes everything

**Dr. Julie Mikles-Schluterman is the Director of the Center for Community
Engagement and Academic Outreach.** She was in the room on 2026-08-06.

Verified on ATU's own Meet the Team page, which lists her as "Director of the
Center for Community Engagement and Academic Outreach" and Professor of
Sociology, Department of Behavioral Sciences, Witherspoon Hall 344, 407 West Q
Street, Russellville, phone (479) 498-6050. Her email, from ATU's news service,
is jmiklesschluterman@atu.edu.

She is also a co-principal investigator on the federal award that created the
center. Her own description of ATU's role in it: "In this effort, we will
establish the Center for Community Engagement and Academic Outreach on the ATU
campus. We will launch interdisciplinary, project-based learning courses and
oversee an on-campus innovation hub."

Three consequences, and they are the reason to read the rest of this document:

1. The person Tal already has an open research ask with personally controls the
   center's grant-making.
2. Every meaningful funding door in this document is a door she either opens or
   stands next to, including one nobody expected.
3. The center exists because of a four-year federal grant whose stated purpose is
   community engagement and workforce outreach, and that grant has a renewal to
   win. The center needs community impact stories. Town Square is one.

Secondary finding, and the one with real dollars behind it: **ATU is an approved
AmeriCorps VISTA sponsoring organization, and Mikles-Schluterman runs that too.**
See opportunity 2 below.

## The top five, and the angle to play

### 1. AmeriCorps VISTA, sponsored by ATU

This is the largest real resource on the list and it was not on anyone's radar.
ATU was approved as a sponsoring organization for AmeriCorps VISTA, with up to
five full-time members serving one-year terms, and Mikles-Schluterman leads the
initiative. VISTA members exist specifically to "build capacity in non-profit
organizations and public agencies." A VISTA member is, in practical terms, a
federally funded full-time person for a year at no cash cost to the host.

The honest constraints. ATU's current VISTA positions are committed elsewhere:
four are county-specific across Pope, Johnson, Yell, and Franklin/Logan, one is
assistant to the center's director, and the stated program goal is "to improve
the college preparedness of students in the five-county target populations."
That is not Town Square's mission. The program year starts 10 June, so the next
realistic entry point is the 2027 cohort, which means this is a fall 2026
conversation for a summer 2027 payoff.

The angle: do not ask for a VISTA member. Ask Mikles-Schluterman whether a
future VISTA position could be scoped around community information access in
Pope County, which is defensibly adjacent to college preparedness because
information access is the mechanism by which families learn about programs at
all. Because ATU is the sponsor, ATU carries the compliance burden and Town
Square's missing 501(c)(3) may not be disqualifying. May. That is unverified and
it is the first thing to ask.

### 2. The IPBL Community-Based Research Grant, with Mikles-Schluterman as PI

Highest-probability money here, and also the smallest: $500 attached to a course.
Do not chase it for the dollars. Chase it because it converts Tal's open research
ask into a funded, catalogued ATU activity with a faculty name attached, and
because the person who decides is the person who already heard the pitch.

The angle: Tal is not asking a favor, he is handing her a ready-made course
project that satisfies her own center's published grant criteria. The grant is
for "a course that involves students in conducting research to generate
knowledge about a community need." The community need is already documented in
ATU's own words from the prior Community Health Needs-Finding Workshop, which
predates Town Square's involvement. The research question is what the loss of a
shared local information channel has cost Russellville. She is the lever, and
she is the only person named in this document who sits on both sides of the
transaction.

### 3. AR-NETWORK, played as a relationship and not an application

There is no public front door where a community organization applies to
AR-NETWORK for money, and none should be invented. What matters is knowing where
the center's money comes from. CCEAO exists because of an $8M NSF E-CORE award
to the Arkansas Research Alliance, announced 21 July 2025, led by PI Sarah
Burnett Smith, distributed over four years with a renewable second $8M term that
ARA has publicly said it intends to pursue.

The angle: renewals are won with evidence of community impact, and that evidence
gets assembled by staff. Town Square is an unusually clean case study, because it
is a working platform with 741 loaded Russellville listings, a running review
queue, and a technical partner who ships daily, rather than a proposal. Position
Town Square as the story AR-NETWORK wants in its renewal package and the center
acquires a self-interested reason to keep pointing course activity and student
labor at it. Mikles-Schluterman is the lever as co-PI. Debra Fithen, as Project
Coordinator, is the person who would actually assemble that reporting, which
makes her the one to hand a clean one-pager to.

### 4. The CCEAO Collaboration Request Form, the quieter second door

Separate from the capstone Microsoft Form and easy to miss. It is open to
organizations, agencies, and nonprofits, and asks for contact details,
organization mission, nonprofit status, collaboration type, and a description of
needs including "specific skills or competencies needed." Checkboxes cover
course-based projects, research collaborations, internships, and hiring. It asks
about nonprofit status but nothing found says nonprofit status is required.

The angle: submitting it puts Town Square in the center's partner pipeline for
everything that is not the CS capstone, specifically the sociology research
collaboration and student interns. It costs one form and it is the mechanism the
center itself directs organizations toward, which means using it reads as
competence rather than as an end run. Fithen is the likely recipient.

### 5. A faculty-PI external grant with Town Square as named community partner

The long game, and the only path here with meaningful dollars. Town Square has no
confirmed 501(c)(3), which closes most foundation doors outright. A faculty PI
closes that gap structurally: the university is the applicant, the university
holds and administers the money, and Town Square is the named community partner
and the site of the work. Mikles-Schluterman for anything sociological or
community-impact framed, Cunningham or Ghosh for anything computing or workforce
framed.

The specific vehicle is not identified and should not be guessed at. The correct
next move is a conversation with ATU's Office of Research and Sponsored Programs,
the pre-award office that locates funding sources, interprets solicitations, and
reviews budgets and proposals, at grants@atu.edu or (479) 968-0398. Greg Crouch
is named in ATU's news service as director of grants and sponsored programs. That
conversation is faculty-initiated, so it still runs through the same three names.

## Dead ends

Checked and ruled out. Do not re-check these.

- **ATU Foundation.** Ruled out. It is single-organization support for ATU
  itself, EIN 71-0561120. Its stated purpose is to "promote the welfare and
  future development of Arkansas Tech University," and its actual grant-making is
  scholarships to ATU students plus institutional support to the university.
  There is no community grant program a non-ATU entity can access. Healthy
  balance sheet, irrelevant to Town Square.
- **https://www.atu.edu/ipbl/contact.php** returns HTTP 404. Live contact
  information is on the Meet the Team page.
- **https://www.atu.edu/ipbl/river-valley-nonprofits.php** returns HTTP 404,
  despite appearing in search results. Would have been useful for identifying a
  fiscal sponsor.
- **https://www.atu.edu/orgs/orsp/pdg.php** returns HTTP 404. The PDG amount is
  not retrievable from the public web and needs a direct ask to ORSP.
- **University of Arkansas Center for Community Engagement (service.uark.edu)**
  dominates search results for this topic. Different institution, Fayetteville,
  irrelevant.
- **The main IPBL landing page** has no grants section at all. Grant detail lives
  only on the About IPBL subpage.
- **The Spark Tank page** is a winners list with no program description, amounts,
  deadline, or eligibility. Program details came from press coverage instead, and
  the answer is that Town Square cannot enter because entry is ATU students only.
- **ATU financial aid grant pages** (Pell, GO! Grant, ArFuture, HEERF, CARES,
  CRRSAA, ARPA) are all student aid. Not applicable, and the pandemic-era ones
  are closed or depleted.
- **AR-NETWORK public materials** describe funding for research jump-starts,
  workshops pairing scientists with entrepreneurs, student internships, and
  teacher externships. No community organization application path is described
  anywhere, and no ATU sub-award figure has been published.

## Open questions for Tal

1. **Can a VISTA member be placed with an organization that has no 501(c)(3),
   when ATU is the sponsor?** This is the highest-value unknown in the document
   and it is one email to Mikles-Schluterman. Everything else here is small money
   or in-kind. This one is a full-time person for a year.
2. **Does Town Square want ATU to hold the money?** A faculty-PI grant means the
   university owns the award and Town Square is a partner rather than a grantee.
   It is the fastest route around the missing 501(c)(3) and it is also a real
   loss of control. Decide before the first conversation, not during it.
3. **Is Town Square a for-profit or a nonprofit, and when does that get
   decided?** It changes eligibility in both directions. For-profit unlocks free
   ASBTDC consulting today. Nonprofit unlocks VISTA hosting and most foundation
   money later. The answer is currently neither, which is the weakest position.
4. **Is anyone in the coalition already a 501(c)(3) willing to act as fiscal
   sponsor?** Main Street Russellville is the strongest candidate, since Main
   Street organizations are typically nonprofits and it has a documented history
   of receiving grants. Russellville Housing Authority is a public agency, which
   is separately useful because VISTA explicitly serves public agencies too.
5. **Should the research ask to Mikles-Schluterman be framed as an IPBL grant
   application or as a larger externally funded study?** Recommendation is both,
   in that order, because the $500 grant is a low-stakes way to get her name
   formally attached before asking for anything bigger.
6. **Is the sociology track a separate capstone submission or an informal
   collaboration?** Still unanswered from the coalition design doc, and it
   determines whether the research ask needs its own submission before early
   September.
