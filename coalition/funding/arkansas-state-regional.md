# Arkansas State and Regional Funding for Town Square

Research current as of 2026-08-07. Scope is Arkansas state, regional, and local
money only. Federal programs and ATU internal funding are covered by separate
research and are deliberately excluded here.

Two facts constrain everything below.

1. **Town Square has no confirmed 501(c)(3) status.** Almost every funder in
   Arkansas requires the applicant to be a 501(c)(3) public charity, a school, a
   hospital, or a government agency. The single most useful structural fact in
   this document: **Russellville Housing Authority is a government agency**,
   which satisfies that requirement at Arkansas Community Foundation, Entergy,
   and the Winthrop Rockefeller Foundation without anyone needing to form a new
   nonprofit.
2. **Nobody in Arkansas funds "a software platform."** They fund programs that
   serve people. Every angle below reframes Town Square as the delivery
   mechanism for a documented community need, using ATU's own language:
   improved access to information, stronger communication about available
   services, transportation coordination, resource mapping, data collection.

Confidence levels: **High** means confirmed on the funder's own website.
**Medium** means confirmed by a credible secondary source but not the funder's
primary page. **Low** or **unverified** means the program appears to exist but a
specific claim could not be confirmed, and is flagged in the notes.

## Top 5, and the angle for each

### 1. Winthrop Rockefeller Foundation

This is the best strategic fit in Arkansas and the only funder on this list
whose stated theory of change actually matches what Town Square is. WRF funds
movement building, community-led initiatives, innovation, and systems
disruption, and explicitly requires that applicants engage the people most
affected in defining the root causes. Town Square's governance design, a local
community committee owning the platform rather than a vendor, is exactly that
argument. Critically, WRF does **not** require the applicant to be a 501(c)(3)
to submit an idea, and it accepts a fiscal sponsor when the initiative is not
led by a nonprofit. The pitch: Russellville's residents cannot find out what
services exist, and the cost of reaching them has been privatized by platforms
that charge per email and per boosted post. Town Square rebuilds the commons as
public infrastructure owned by a community committee, which is a structural fix
rather than another program. It maps directly to "improved access to
information" and "stronger communication about available services." The catch
is speed: WRF's own guidance is to plan for 6 to 12 months, and the process
starts with a Funding Request Form and a conversation, not a proposal. Start
this now precisely because it is slow.

### 2. Arkansas Community Foundation, Pope County affiliate, Giving Tree Grants

This is the most winnable local money and the most credible signal to every
other funder on the list, because the decision is made by a committee of Pope
County residents. The affiliate awarded 17 grants in 2026, weighted toward
basic human needs, which tells you the frame: this is not a technology grant,
it is a "people in Pope County cannot find the help that already exists" grant.
The angle is resource mapping and service navigation, delivered as a Town
Square directory built on the 741 real Russellville listings already loaded,
with the Housing Authority and Emerging Hope Therapy as the named beneficiaries
whose clients get a working referral path. This maps to "resource mapping" and
"stronger communication about available services." The applicant should be
Russellville Housing Authority, which qualifies as a government agency and
already carries the credibility of serving the exact population ARCF's local
committee funds. The timing question is unresolved and is the top open item
below.

### 3. Entergy, Open Grants and Micro Grants

Arkansas Nuclear One is in Russellville and Entergy states it awards more than
$100,000 a year to local nonprofits in the Arkansas River Valley, plus more
than $230,000 to the River Valley United Way. Applications are accepted on a
rolling basis with no deadline, which makes this the lowest-friction money on
the list and the right first ask. Entergy's Open Grants categories include
community improvement and enrichment, education and literacy, and poverty
solutions and social services. The pitch is community improvement plainly
stated: one public place where every organization in Russellville can publish
what it is doing, at no cost, so residents stop missing services they qualify
for. The Micro Grant path (up to $1,000, rolling) is worth filing immediately
as a low-stakes way to establish a relationship and a grant record before
asking for anything larger. Entergy's own guidance is to submit at least three
months before funds are needed, and its staff encourage contacting the local
contributions coordinator first, so the real first action is a phone call to
ANO community relations, not a form.

### 4. Blue & You Foundation for a Healthier Arkansas, Mini Grants

Open right now, closing 2026-08-31, $1,000 to $5,000, decisions in September.
The honest assessment: the category fit is weak. Blue & You Mini Grants fund
five named categories, and the only one Town Square can credibly reach is
behavioral health. The viable framing is narrow and specific rather than
platform-wide: a behavioral health resource directory for the River Valley,
built and maintained on Town Square, so that people in crisis and the
organizations referring them can find current, verified provider information in
one place. Emerging Hope Therapy is the natural program partner and the
strongest voice on why the current state is broken. The applicant must be a
501(c)(3), government entity, church, or school, so if Emerging Hope is a
for-profit practice the applicant reverts to the Housing Authority. Small money,
fast decision, and a real grant on the record before the ATU capstone teams are
assigned in mid-September.

### 5. Arkansas Farm Bureau Foundation, General Funding Request

Closes 2026-08-15, eight days out. The Foundation's mission language is about
strengthening ties between agricultural and rural communities across Arkansas,
and its separate Community Grant Program funds projects that invest in rural
communities. Pope County is genuinely rural outside the Russellville city
limits, and the strongest version of this pitch is the part of Town Square that
serves people who are furthest from information: residents in Dover, Hector,
Atkins, and the unincorporated parts of the county who have no local news, no
city communications office, and thin broadband. Amounts are not published and
the General Funding Request appears to be oriented toward events, sponsorships,
and donations rather than program grants, so calibrate expectations down. This
is a fast, cheap shot worth taking because the window is open and the form is a
JotForm, not a proposal. Verify the amount range with Amanda Williams before
investing real effort.

## Dead ends

- **Delta Regional Authority.** Pope County is not in DRA's Arkansas service
  area. The Arkansas county list runs Arkansas, Ashley, Baxter, Bradley,
  Calhoun, Chicot, Clay, Cleveland, Craighead, Crittenden, Cross, Dallas, Desha,
  Drew, Fulton, Grant, Greene, Independence, Izard, Jackson, Jefferson,
  Lawrence, Lee, Lincoln, Lonoke, Marion, Mississippi, Monroe, Ouachita,
  Phillips, Poinsett, Prairie, Pulaski, Randolph, Searcy, Sharp, St. Francis,
  Stone, Union, Van Buren, White, and Woodruff. Pope is absent. Note that DRA
  does include a Pope County in **Illinois**, which is a common source of false
  positives in grant databases. Close this permanently.
- **Arkansas digital equity and digital inclusion money.** This is the most
  painful dead end and the one most likely to be recommended by someone who has
  not checked. Arkansas had an approved Digital Skills and Opportunity Plan and
  was awarded $10,161,429.01 under the State Digital Equity Capacity Grant
  Program in December 2024. In May 2025 the U.S. Department of Commerce
  terminated the Digital Equity Act programs, rescinding grants to 56 states and
  territories and eliminating roughly $30.8 million in digital skills funding
  across Arkansas, including the $10.1 million already sent to the state
  broadband office, a further $7 million planned, $10.7 million for UAMS, and
  about $3 million in competitive funds. Arkansas Broadband Director Glen Howie
  said the office was evaluating alternative funding sources. No replacement
  program was found as of 2026-08-07. This is worth a single email to the
  broadband office rather than an application.
- **BEAD non-infrastructure funds.** BEAD in Arkansas is infrastructure
  deployment awarded to ISPs, and the non-deployment flexibility that once
  existed was narrowed by the June 2025 NTIA restructuring notice. Arkansas
  rescinded and re-ran its subgrantee selections. There is no community
  applicant path here.
- **AEDC Rural Community Grant Program and Rural Services Block Grant.**
  Ineligible twice over. RCGP is limited to communities under 3,000 population,
  and Russellville is roughly ten times that. Both programs fund construction,
  renovation, parks, storm shelters, fire stations, and fire equipment, not
  software or programs. RSBG additionally requires an AEDC-certified grant
  writer.
- **Main Street Arkansas grants generally.** Design, building rehabilitation,
  streetscape, and permanent public art. No technology, communications, or
  program category exists.
- **Arkansas Community Foundation Arts in Arkansas Fund.** Closed. It was a
  two-year, $5,000 general operating support cohort for small arts nonprofits
  with budgets under $500,000, funded by the Alice L. Walton Foundation, the
  Walton Family Foundation, and WRF. Awards were announced by June 2025.
- **Arkansas Arts Council General Operating Support and Major Arts Partners.**
  Require an established arts nonprofit with $50,000+ or $1 million+ in income.
  No coalition partner qualifies.
- **Arkansas Municipal League.** Not a grantmaker. It brokers free access to the
  National League of Cities GrantFinder database for Arkansas cities. Useful as
  a research tool for the City of Russellville, not a source of funds. Contact
  named in coverage is Dylan McLeod.
- **Arvest Foundation.** No public application portal was found. Giving appears
  to be routed through local and regional bank presidents rather than an open
  cycle. Treat as a relationship ask, not an application.
- **Arkansas Valley Electric Cooperative RoundUp for Change.** Funds go to
  individual co-op members in need, not to organizations. Also, AVECC's
  territory is Crawford, Logan, Johnson and westward, not Pope.
- **Arkansas State Library / LSTA.** Arkansas appears to use its Grants to
  States allocation for statewide projects rather than a standing competitive
  local subgrant cycle, and no open 2026 subgrant was found. LSTA funds are also
  themselves at risk, with the Arkansas Library Association warning about the
  loss of $2.16 million annually. Low probability, but the Pope County Library
  System is still worth adding to the coalition for reasons other than money.
- **HumanitiesAR institutional risk.** On 2025-04-02 the NEH terminated all
  federal funding to HumanitiesAR, and its executive director said the
  organization could close without new funding. A $15 million Mellon Foundation
  grant to the Federation of State Humanities Councils sent $200,000 to
  HumanitiesAR as immediate unrestricted support. The monthly $750 to $1,000
  grants above are still listed on its site, but treat the larger major and
  mini-grant categories as unstable until confirmed by staff at 501-353-0349.

## Open questions for Tal

1. **Does the Pope County ARCF affiliate accept applications in the July to
   August cycle, or only January to February?** This is the highest-leverage
   unknown in the document and it is one phone call to Madelyn Ginsberg at
   479-315-6427. If yes, there is an eight-day sprint starting now. If no, the
   Pope County Giving Tree becomes a January 2027 target with five months to
   build the relationship, which is arguably the better outcome.
2. **Who signs as the applicant?** Russellville Housing Authority is a
   government agency, which satisfies the eligibility bar at ARCF, Entergy, and
   WRF without a new nonprofit or a fiscal sponsor. Has Donna Smith been asked
   whether the Housing Authority is willing to be the named applicant, and does
   the Housing Authority have grant administration capacity? Nothing else in
   this document matters until this is answered.
3. **Is Main Street Russellville a 501(c)(3), and is it an accredited Main
   Street Arkansas program?** Both are unverified and both change its usefulness
   as an applicant. Accreditation specifically gates the Main Street Arkansas
   grants.
4. **Is Emerging Hope Therapy a nonprofit or a for-profit practice?** This
   determines whether the Blue & You behavioral health path is real or whether
   it has to route through the Housing Authority.
5. **Is Town Square willing to be a line item inside a partner's program budget
   rather than the named grantee?** Every funder here funds programs delivered
   to residents. The winning structure is almost certainly "Housing Authority
   runs a resident information and referral program, and Town Square is the
   platform it runs on," not "fund Town Square."
6. **Fiscal sponsor now, or 501(c)(3) later?** WRF explicitly accepts a fiscal
   sponsor. Standing one up is faster than incorporating, but it means a partner
   holds the money. This is a governance decision that touches the community
   committee design, not just a paperwork decision.
7. **Should the City of Russellville and Pope County be recruited into the
   coalition?** They are absent from the current roster. Their presence would
   unlock the West Central Arkansas Planning and Development District as a
   channel to state CDBG money and would make the Trendsetter Cities entry
   possible. WCAPDD covers Clark, Conway, Garland, Hot Spring, Johnson,
   Montgomery, Perry, Pike, Pope, and Yell counties, and its stated job is
   walking cities and counties through grant applications and then administering
   the projects. Contact is 501-525-7577, Hot Springs.
8. **How much of this should wait until after mid-September?** ATU contacts
   selected capstone partners in mid-September. A funded coalition is a stronger
   capstone partner, but a distracted Tal is a worse proposal writer. The two
   things that should not wait regardless are the Ginsberg call and the WRF
   Funding Request Form, because one is time-boxed and the other takes 6 to 12
   months.

## Scope note

The $8 million AR-NETWORK award that funds the ATU Center for Community
Engagement and the Co-Create Innovation Hub's ATU ThinkSpace is a National
Science Foundation grant administered through a statewide collaboration, with
Dr. Julie Mikles-Schluterman as a co-principal investigator. It is federal
money and ATU-internal, so it sits outside this document's scope and is covered
by the separate AR-NETWORK and ATU research.
