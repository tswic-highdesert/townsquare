# AR-NETWORK

Research completed 2026-08-07. Every claim below is sourced. Anything I could
not verify is labeled "unverified" and named explicitly.

## What AR-NETWORK actually is

**AR-NETWORK is the Arkansas Nexus for Excellence in Technology, Workforce,
Outreach and Research Knowledge.** It is an NSF-funded statewide research
capacity program, not a health network, not a rural network, and not a
broadband program. **Confidence: HIGH.** This is settled, not inferred.

**Who runs it.** The Arkansas Research Alliance (ARA), a Little Rock nonprofit
whose stated purpose is "supporting job-creating research in Arkansas."
Contact: info@aralliance.org, (501) 450-7818. Program site:
<https://www.ar-network.org> (also serves at arnetwork.aralliance.org).

**The three cores**, quoted from the AR-NETWORK About page:

1. **Administration Core.** Coordination and oversight.
2. **Economic Development Core.** "Foster industry-academic partnerships,
   provide federal commercialization funding support, **establish innovation
   hubs, and host needs-finding workshops**."
3. **Workforce Development Core.** "Develop programs aligning educational
   pathways with workforce needs, **establish the Center for Community
   Engagement and Academic Outreach (CCEAO)**, and facilitate community college
   research experiences."

**This is the finding that matters.** The two things Tal walked into on
2026-08-06, a needs-finding workshop and the CCEAO, are both named line items in
AR-NETWORK's own core structure. The Co-Create Innovation Hub is the third
(innovation hubs). Tal did not stumble into a community meeting. He walked into
the field-level output of an $8M federal cooperative agreement, and every person
who convened it is paid, in part, by it.

**Relationship to ATU and CCEAO.** Direct and formal, not incidental.
Dr. Julie Mikles-Schluterman is simultaneously (a) co-PI on award 2438206,
(b) co-lead of the AR-NETWORK Workforce Development Core, and (c) Director of
the ATU Center for Community Engagement and Academic Outreach. ATU's own CCEAO
page states: "CCEAO is supported by the NSF EPSCoR-funded AR-NETWORK initiative"
and "**CCEAO serves as the implementation arm for many AR-NETWORK
partnerships.**" Source: <https://www.atu.edu/ipbl/index.php>

CCEAO's operating vehicle is **IPBL**, Interdisciplinary Project-Based Learning:
courses that are interdisciplinary, project-based, and community-engaged. The CS
capstone pipeline in the coalition design doc is one lane of a larger machine
CCEAO runs. CCEAO also oversees ATU's AmeriCorps VISTA program and runs Spark
Tank, a student pitch competition with non-profit and for-profit divisions.

**Five strategic focus areas** (these are the boxes any AR-NETWORK activity has
to fit into): Energy Systems and Power Electronics; Advanced Materials and
Manufacturing; Precision Agriculture and Food Systems; **Health and
Biosciences**; **Data and Supply Chain Analytics**.

**One naming discrepancy worth knowing.** ATU's CCEAO page expands the acronym
as "Arkansas Network for Engaged Workforce, Outreach, Research, & Knowledge."
NSF and ARA both use "Arkansas Nexus for Excellence in Technology, Workforce,
Outreach and Research Knowledge." NSF's is authoritative. Use it in writing.

no community mini-grant, no pilot fund, no community RFP, and no open

abstract. What AR-NETWORK spends money on is faculty, students, convenings, and
hub space. The value to Town Square is real but it is in-kind, not cash.

**Is the needs-finding workshop phase one of something with implementation money
behind it?** Partly, and the honest answer is more useful than a hopeful one.

Yes, it is a funded phase. The NSF abstract lists "hosting workshops to identify
industry needs" as a funded activity, and the Economic Development Core owns it.
AR-NETWORK's own summit page describes the same format at the statewide level:
"facilitated industry needs-finding discussions" whose purpose is to "surface
priority problem areas, **inform future use-inspired research efforts**, and
identify pathways for collaboration."

But phase two is not a community implementation grant. Phase two is a **research
proposal**. E-CORE is explicitly the ecosystem-building half of NSF EPSCoR and
does not fund research. The research half is **E-RISE RII**, and Arkansas
becoming an E-CORE jurisdiction is what unlocked it. That is the $8M that
follows the needs-finding. It is applied for by an institution, on an August
deadline, and a community platform participates as a named partner or
subawardee, never as the lead unless it has 501(c)(3) status.

Practical read: the 2026-08-11 E-RISE deadline is four days out and unreachable.
The real target is **2027-08-10**, which is a full year to become the obvious
community partner on an Arkansas E-RISE proposal.

## The angle

**The documented needs list is the whole play, and it is stronger than it looks.**

The invitation's needs list, "improved access to information, stronger
communication about available services, transportation coordination, resource
mapping, data collection," is not a community wishlist. It is the recorded output
of an activity NSF is paying ARA to perform, inside a cooperative agreement that
reports annually to a federal program officer. Someone at ATU has to write up
what came out of that workshop and what happened next. Right now the answer to
"what happened next" is nothing.

Town Square is a working answer to four of those five items, already built, with
741 Russellville listings loaded. That is not a proposal. That is a deliverable
that showed up for free.

**What to actually ask for, in order:**

1. **Be the named outcome of the workshop.** This costs nothing and is worth
   more than a small grant. Ask Mikles-Schluterman to log Town Square as the
   community response to the needs-finding workshop in AR-NETWORK reporting.
   Federal cooperative agreements live and die on demonstrated community impact,
   and a live platform is a better annual-report line than a summary of a
   meeting. This is a favor Tal is doing for her, not the reverse. Frame it that
   way.
2. **Submit the CCEAO Collaboration Request Form, not just the CS capstone
   form.** These are different doors. The capstone form gets one CS team,
   competitively. The Collaboration Request Form is CCEAO's front door for the
   whole IPBL portfolio, which is interdisciplinary by design and is the lane
   sociology, journalism, and business students come through. The spring 2026
   Russellville recycling course, which paired sociology with journalism to build
   community-facing communications, is the exact template. Town Square wants that
   course, not only the CS capstone.
3. **Get on the Population Health summit.** It is the one 2026 summit still TBD.
   Population Health is one of ARA's five strategic areas and is the statewide
   framing under which a community information platform is legible to this
   program. Contact is ARA Project Manager Allison Liddell via
   info@aralliance.org. The summit format has a "Research Showcase" and
   "Grand Rounds" slot. A Russellville-hosted Population Health summit at
   Co-Create, with Town Square as the local case, is a plausible ask that serves
   ARA's own calendar problem.
4. **Position for E-RISE 2027-08-10.** Not as applicant. As the community
   infrastructure partner named in someone else's proposal, most likely an ATU
   or UAMS-led one. Twelve months is exactly enough lead time.

**If the health framing comes up, it is an asset.** AR-NETWORK's Health and
Biosciences and Data and Supply Chain Analytics areas both cover this cleanly.
The mapping to use in writing:

- **Social determinants of health.** Transportation coordination and access to
  services are textbook SDOH domains. A resident who cannot find out that a
  service exists does not receive it. That is an access barrier, and it is the
  one Town Square removes.
- **Health information access.** "Improved access to information" and "stronger
  communication about available services" are health communication problems when
  the services are health services, and in Russellville several of them are. The
  Housing Authority and Emerging Hope Therapy were both in the room.
- **Care coordination and resource mapping.** A structured, current directory of
  local services is the substrate every referral workflow and community health
  worker depends on. Town Square's 741 listings are that substrate.
- **Community health worker infrastructure.** Vouching and residency
  verification produce a trusted local network, which is the same asset a CHW
  program needs and usually has to build from scratch.
- **Data collection.** Town Square generates typed records of what residents look
  for and cannot find. That is measurable unmet-need data, which is precisely
  what a research proposal needs and what a needs-finding workshop cannot
  produce.

## Dead ends

Things I ruled out, with the evidence, so nobody re-litigates them.

- **Not a health network.** Ruled out. AR-NETWORK is an NSF EPSCoR award through
  the Office of Integrative Activities, award 2438206. Not NIH, not AHRQ, not
  HRSA. The "Community Health Needs-Finding Workshop" title borrows the
  Economic Development Core's needs-finding workshop format and applies it to
  population health, one of five strategic areas.
- **Not Arkansas Rural Network, not a Delta or ARC or USDA rural network, not a
  state broadband or telehealth network.** All ruled out by the NSF award record.
- **No community mini-grant or pilot fund exists.** Checked the program site,
  all five program subpages, the full news archive back to January 2025, and the
  NSF abstract. Three of the five programs (Internships, Externships,
  Collaborative Initiatives) are literally marked "Opportunities Coming Soon"
  with no dates and unanswered FAQ stubs. Do not build a plan on them.
- **E-CORE funds cannot flow to Town Square directly.** It is a cooperative
  agreement to ARA for ecosystem building. There is no subaward mechanism for a
  community project, and the cores buy staff, convenings, hubs, and courses.
- **ARA Impact Grants are closed to this.** Lead PI must be at an ARA partner
  institution, and **ATU is not one**. Also requires an industry letter and a
  20 percent match. No 4.0 RFP is published.
- **ERL is faculty-only.** $30,000 per year for two years, awarded to
  individual Arkansas researchers. Town Square cannot hold one.
- **Spark Tank is students-only.** ATU student pitch competition. Not a path.
- **E-RISE 2026 is unreachable.** Deadline 2026-08-11, four days out, and it
  needs an institutional lead. Target 2027.

One correction to the coalition design doc: it lists the Co-Create Innovation
Hub at **215 South Main Street**. Every source puts it at **215 South Arkansas
Avenue**, Russellville. ATU's own campus address is 215 West O Street, which may
be where the confusion came from. Worth fixing before anything goes out in
writing.

   workshop, and is there a written report?** I could not find one published. If
