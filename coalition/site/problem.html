Russellville, Arkansas August 2026

The problem

Russellville does not have a town square anymore.

Everything a resident might want to know about their town exists somewhere.

  • City hall The council decided something Tuesday.
  • A nonprofit Volunteers needed Saturday.
  • Main Street A new business opened.
  • Somebody’s feed A podcast about the river.

None of it is in the same place.

Most of it never reaches the person who would have cared.

That is not four problems. It is one problem showing up as different symptoms.

What people actually say

On August 6, 2026, community members sat in a room at the Co-Create Innovation Hub and were asked, one at a time, to name a problem technology might solve. Before Town Square was ever mentioned, people named these.

  1. “

    People are not involved.

  2. “

    People do not know what is happening.

  3. “

    It costs money just to send an email, and no organization here has the budget to communicate effectively in the landscape we actually live in.

Arkansas Tech University has documented the same thing, summarizing an earlier AR-NETWORK needs-finding workshop.

  • Improved access to information
  • Stronger communication about available services
  • Transportation coordination
  • Resource mapping
  • Data collection

We have been hearing a version of this for a decade, and louder every year since Covid. Ask the same question in any town in America and you will get these same answers back. That is not a coincidence, and it is not a Russellville failing.

Nobody needs convincing that the problem is real. We have all been living in it. What has been missing is a solution anyone has actually dialed in.
