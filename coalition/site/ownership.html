Russellville, Arkansas August 2026

Ownership

This one belongs to the town.

We did not pick the name to be cute.

In the early days, Facebook called itself the digital town square. They were right about what they were building.

They were also the ones who owned it, and they sell it.

A town square is supposed to be a place for commerce. People sell things there. That is part of what one is for.

The difference is who decides. When the square is owned two thousand miles away, somebody else decides who gets a spot in it, who gets to sell, which services you hear about, which resources ever reach you.

They decide on a quarterly basis, for reasons that have nothing to do with here.

We are already paying for this.

There is a person on the other end of every message that does not arrive, and some of them do not get another chance. People have been hurt by this. Some have died.

Your neighbors follow the food pantry for a reason. They needed help. The pantry posts, and they do not see it unless the pantry pays. That is the machine working the way it was built to work.

A machine that sells the space in front of you always has to hold some of it back, or there is nothing left to sell.

The organizations squeezed out first are the ones with the smallest budgets, and they are often doing the most urgent work.

  • The crisis line.
  • The shelter with beds open tonight.
  • The clinic with a closing enrollment window.
  • The warming center.
  • The pantry.

And the wider damage is harder and slower to see. Civic participation thins. People stop knowing their neighbors. They stop showing up. They stop trusting institutions.

Nobody has or can put a number on what this has cost our towns, and we are not going to wait for one. So we are building one that nobody can buy.

Open source, community-owned infrastructure.
