Russellville, Arkansas August 2026

How we got here

A community’s most valuable asset is its attention.

For most of a century that attention had somewhere to live.

What we had
  • The paper landed on the porch.
  • The radio was on in the truck.

That infrastructure was analog, physical, local, and imperfect, and it worked. If something mattered, it reached you, and the institution that carried it lived in the same town you did.

We gave that away.

Who we gave it to

Not to another local institution. We handed it to Zuckerberg and friends, to companies two thousand miles from here that rent our attention back to us and decide, day to day, who sees what.

A nonprofit in Russellville now has to buy access to people who already asked to hear from it.

The old infrastructure did not simply fade out. It was replaced by something that was never built to serve a town in the first place.

And it was doing two jobs, not one

The paper was the town’s megaphone and the town’s archive.

01

It reached people today.

Whatever mattered on a Tuesday got in front of the town on Tuesday, without anyone having to go looking for it.

What replaced it does this job badly.

02

It wrote down what happened for tomorrow.

A shelf of back issues is a town keeping its own record, one ordinary day at a time, without calling it a history project.

What replaced it does not do this job at all. A Facebook post from 2019 is functionally unfindable.

We did not just trade a distribution channel. We traded an archive for a machine designed to forget.
