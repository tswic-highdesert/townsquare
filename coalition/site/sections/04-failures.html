The usual answers

Why the usual answers do not work

Each of these has been tried, here and in towns like ours. They do not fail vaguely. They fail in specific, nameable ways.

Facebook groups

Rented land

There is an algorithm between an organization and the people who explicitly asked to hear from it, and the terms change whenever someone else decides they should. A group that took three years to build can lose its reach in a week.

Local news

Thinning everywhere

Thinning here, and thinning everywhere else too. What remains cannot cover a school board, a quorum court, a planning commission, and forty nonprofits.

The city website

Built for the statute

Built to satisfy a legal requirement to publish, not to help a resident understand.

A PDF posted on time is compliance. It is not communication.

Email lists

Expensive and siloed

One-way and siloed by design. Every organization pays separately to reach an overlapping set of the same people, and none of those lists ever help a resident discover an organization they had not already heard of.

Another app

Most die

Every community app so far is a product people consume. It lives or dies on whether one organization can keep feeding it forever.
