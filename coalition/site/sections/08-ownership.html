Ownership

Nobody should own a town square.

We did not pick the name to be cute.

In the early days, Facebook described itself as the digital town square. They were right about what they were building.

They were just also the ones who owned it, and they sell it.

This is not an argument against free enterprise. Businesses should make money.

But a town square is not supposed to be a product. It is the one place where a community sees itself, and when that place has been bought and paid for by interests two thousand miles away, the community does not get to decide what happens in it anymore.

Somebody else does, on a quarterly basis, for reasons that have nothing to do with this town.

And this has already cost us something.

This part is not theoretical.

When the only reliable way to reach your neighbors is a channel you have to pay for, the organizations that get squeezed out first are the ones with the smallest budgets. Those are often the ones doing the most urgent work.

  • The crisis line.
  • The shelter with beds open tonight.
  • The clinic with a closing enrollment window.
  • The warming center.
  • The pantry.

A nonprofit that cannot get a message out is not merely inconvenienced. There is a person on the other end of that message who needed it, and consequences that follow when it never arrives. Everyone who works in this field has watched it happen and can name the case.

The wider damage is slower and harder to see. Civic participation thins. People stop knowing their neighbors, stop showing up, stop trusting institutions they no longer hear from directly. That is a real deterioration of civic fabric, and it has real costs.

A research ask for Arkansas Tech

We cannot currently tell you what those costs are here, in numbers. Nobody has measured it in this community.

That is one of the things we would like ATU to help with: a study that quantifies what the loss of a shared local information channel has actually done to Russellville, and what could be done about it.

Naming the harm precisely is the first step to fixing it, and it is exactly the kind of question a university should be answering.

So here is how this one is held.

  1. 01 Open source

    Another town can take it, run it, and adapt it without asking anyone. Russellville is town one, not the only town.

  2. 02 A local committee

    Governance sits with a local Town Square committee, not a company and not a person, which is also what an adopting town stands up for itself.

  3. 03 Vouching

    Residency is verified by residents vouching for residents. We store that someone is verified and never store where they live.

  4. 04 Light touch

    This is a neutral place, not a publisher. Every record is versioned, attributed, and reversible, which is what lets it stay that way.

None of that makes it un-ownable forever. It makes it owned by the town instead of by us.
