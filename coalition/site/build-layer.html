What people make of it

Everything Town Square becomes starts with somebody here

People here already have ideas about what this town needs. What they have never had is a place to put one where the town would find it.

Watchdog

What is bearing down on us

The license plate readers. The data centers. Whatever is next. Tracked by the people who actually live here.

Civic

What the council decided

Plain English, every meeting, so following your own local government does not require reading a PDF written for the record.

Service

Who needs help Saturday

Every local nonprofit posting volunteer shifts in one place, free, instead of each one paying to email the same 200 people.

Commerce

Buy, sell, and trade

A marketplace that belongs to the town, instead of one more thing happening in somebody else's app.

Their own thing

A podcast, a cause, a business

Promote it, grow it, and yes, make money doing it. That is allowed and it is supposed to be.

Delight

The best tamales in town

Not everything has to be civic. Some of it just has to be worth arguing about.

We are not building those. We are building the ground they stand on, and the place the town finds them.

The kind of thing a resident does with this

Someone asks the city how many license plate readers are up on Arkansas Avenue, and never gets a straight number. Three weekends later there are 41 of them on a map, the map lives on Town Square, and anyone in town can add the one they drove past this morning.

She had not written code before April. She works the desk at the library. What she needed was never a developer. It was somewhere to put the thing she made, and neighbors who would actually find it.

That is the whole shape of this layer. The town supplies the curiosity. Town Square supplies the ground it can stand on.

Marta Keener is a representative resident from the design work, not someone we have met. The screens are from the working product.
